## 2. String Harmonics

### Question
A guitar string is 64 cm long and has a fundamental frequency (one antinode) of 330 Hz. What is the speed of the wave on this string?

### Solution

In the fundamental mode (one antinode), the length of the string corresponds to half a wavelength:

$$
L = \frac{\lambda}{2}
$$

So:

$$
\lambda = 2L
$$

Convert length to meters:

$$
L = 64 \text{ cm} = 0.64 \text{ m}
$$

Thus:

$$
\lambda = 2 \times 0.64 = 1.28 \text{ m}
$$

Now use the wave equation:

$$
v = \lambda f
$$

Substitute values:

$$
v = 1.28 \times 330 = 422.4 \text{ m/s}
$$

### Final Answer

- $v \approx 422.4 \ \text{m/s}$

The relation $\lambda = 2L$ comes from the fact that in the fundamental mode there is one antinode, meaning the string contains half of a full wavelength.
