## 1. Wave Properties

**Question:**  
A sound wave in air has a frequency of \(440\,\text{Hz}\). If the speed of sound in air is \(343\,\text{m/s}\), what is its wavelength? What is its wavelength in water, where the speed of sound is \(1482\,\text{m/s}\)?

### Solution

We use the basic wave relation:

\[
v = \lambda f
\]

where:

- \(v\) is the wave speed,
- \(\lambda\) is the wavelength,
- \(f\) is the frequency.

So:

\[
\lambda = \frac{v}{f}
\]

### In air

\[
\lambda_{air} = \frac{343}{440}
\]

\[
\lambda_{air} \approx 0.78\,\text{m}
\]

### In water

\[
\lambda_{water} = \frac{1482}{440}
\]

\[
\lambda_{water} \approx 3.37\,\text{m}
\]

### Final Answer

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{air} \approx 0.78\,\text{m}}
\]

\[
\boxed{\lambda_{water} \approx 3.37\,\text{m}}
\]

The frequency stays the same because it is determined by the source, but the wavelength changes because the wave speed changes in different media.
