## 1. Wave Properties

### Question
A sound wave in air has a frequency of 440 Hz. If the speed of sound in air is 343 m/s, what is its wavelength? What is its wavelength in water, where the speed of sound is 1482 m/s?

### Solution

We use the basic wave relation:

$$
v = \lambda f
$$

where:
- $v$ is the wave speed,
- $\lambda$ is the wavelength,
- $f$ is the frequency.

So:

$$
\lambda = \frac{v}{f}
$$

### In air

$$
\lambda_{\text{air}} = \frac{343}{440} \approx 0.78 \ \text{m}
$$

### In water

$$
\lambda_{\text{water}} = \frac{1482}{440} \approx 3.37 \ \text{m}
$$

### Final Answer

- $\lambda_{\text{air}} \approx 0.78 \ \text{m}$
- $\lambda_{\text{water}} \approx 3.37 \ \text{m}$

The frequency stays the same because it is determined by the source, but the wavelength changes because the wave speed changes in different media.
